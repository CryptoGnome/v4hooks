# v4hooks full catalog

## Angstrom (angstrom)
LP-defending DEX that gates swaps through a staked validator set.
Angstrom uses a Uniswap v4 hook so only staked Angstrom validators can execute swaps, turning the pool into an onchain DEX with an explicit sequencing rule. The stated goal is to defend LPs from toxic flow by refusing unauthenticated takers instead of relying on a static fee.

Pick this sheet if you want MEV protection at the hook layer rather than an RFQ overlay. Verify validator economics, audits, and the live address on hooklist. A gated swap callback is a powerful foot-gun if the allowlist bricks retail flow — read the spec before you point a frontend at it.

## Bunni (bunni)
Shapeshifting LP manager that pairs swap fees with lending yield.
Bunni is an LP management hook for Uniswap v4 that rehypothecates idle liquidity into lending vaults while still collecting swap fees. The job it solves is capital efficiency: concentrated liquidity that would otherwise sit idle can earn a base APY, then snap back when the pool needs depth.

This is the listing to open if you searched for a v4 hook for LP management or rehypothecation rather than a raw pool address. Confirm current vault integrations and audits yourself. v4hooks does not certify Bunni’s risk; it only records that the product exists as a hook you can evaluate.

## Geomean oracle (geomean-oracle)
Turn a Uniswap v4 pool into a geometric-mean price oracle.
The geomean oracle hook records pool prices so downstream contracts can read a time-weighted geometric mean instead of a spot tick. That is the v4 version of “use Uniswap as an oracle,” with hooks writing observations on swap rather than a separate accumulator contract bolted on.

Oracle hooks are easy to misuse: low-liquidity pools, truncated windows, and manipulation across the observation interval all still apply. Read Uniswap’s oracle research and verify the deployed flags. v4hooks lists the pattern so agents can find it; you still have to choose a pool that is safe to read.

## Limit order hook (limit-order)
Place onchain limit orders that fill when the pool trades through a tick.
Uniswap’s v4 limit-order example lets a user rest liquidity at a tick so a swap that crosses that price fills the order. It is the default answer to “does v4 have native limit orders?” — yes, as a hook, not as a core PoolManager feature.

Use this when you want an onchain resting order rather than an intent sent to a solver. The example contract is a starting point; production limit-order hooks add cancellations, partial fills, and gas accounting. Check the live address and flags on Uniswap/hooklist before you post size.

## New York trading hours (trading-days)
Revert swaps while New York equity markets are closed.
horsefacts’ trading-days hook refuses pool actions outside New York market hours. It is the canonical “RWA hours” example: a Uniswap v4 hook that encodes a calendar so onchain FX or equity-like pairs do not trade through the US close.

Use it as prior art for compliance calendars, not as a drop-in for every asset. Timezone, holidays, and L2 sequencer quirks all matter. ## TWAMM (twamm)
Time-weighted average market maker for slicing large orders onchain.
The Uniswap v4 TWAMM example hook executes large orders over time instead of dumping them into a single swap. Searchers looking for “uniswap v4 twamm hook” want this pattern: virtual orders accrue between blocks, then settle against the pool, reducing price impact and some sandwich surface.

The reference lives in v4-periphery example contracts and Uniswap’s TWAMM research post. Production forks exist; this sheet is the canonical idea, not a promise that a specific address is the one Uniswap routes. Verify bytecode and flags on hooklist before you schedule a TWAMM order.
